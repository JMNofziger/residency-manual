**Key 2026 legal changes to build into the manual (verify against Narodne novine text directly before publishing):**
- Single Permit system (combined residence + work permit)
- Employer decision timeline extended from 30 to 90 days
- Workers may change employer after 6 months without a new permit
- Permitted unemployment period extended to 3 months (6 months if held permit >2 years)
- Mandatory A1-level Croatian language + Latin script test within 1 year, required for permit *extension*
- New Form 17a (employer compliance/liability form) and housing standards for employer-provided accommodation
- Visa-required nationals must show medical/vaccination proof from first application (no grace period)
- Blue Card validity extended 24 → 48 months
- Student permits extended up to 3 years

## TRACK 2 — U.S. Citizens Specifically

Note: Croatia does not have a bilateral treaty granting U.S. citizens different *substantive* rights than other third-country nationals — the underlying law (Zakon o strancima) applies the same way. What differs for U.S. citizens is (1) visa-free 90/180-day entry, (2) which consulates process applications, and (3) which U.S.-side documents (FBI background check, etc.) are typically requested. Keep this track focused on those practical differences rather than duplicating Track 1's legal content.

## Structural recommendation for future nationality expansion

Keep a **Track 1 (nationality-neutral legal/process core)** and a **Track N (nationality-specific appendix)** for each new country, where the appendix only needs to cover:
1. Visa-free entry terms (if any) and applicable consulates
2. That country's embassy/consulate guidance page in Croatia
3. Country-specific supporting documents (background check equivalent, apostille rules, etc.)

This avoids duplicating the MUP/HZZ legal content per nationality — only the entry-and-documentation layer changes.
